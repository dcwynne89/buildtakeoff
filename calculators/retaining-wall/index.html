How to Calculate Materials for a Retaining Wall

Retaining walls hold back soil and prevent erosion on sloped properties. Accurate material estimation requires knowing the block dimensions, wall size, and whether drainage and reinforcement are needed.

The Block Count Formula

Blocks per course = Wall Length × 12 ÷ Block Face Width. Total courses = Wall Height × 12 ÷ Block Face Height. Total blocks = Blocks per course × Courses.

Example: A 30 ft long × 3 ft tall wall using standard 8" face blocks = (30 × 12/8) × (3 × 12/4) = 45 × 9 = 405 blocks.

Gravity vs. Reinforced Walls

  • Gravity walls (under 4 ft) — Rely on weight and setback alone to resist soil pressure
  • Reinforced walls (over 4 ft) — Require geogrid layers extending into the retained soil
  • Most municipalities require engineering for walls over 4 feet tall
  • Geogrid layers typically placed every 2 courses (8"–12") for walls 4–8 ft tall
  • Geogrid extends into retained soil at least 60% of the wall height

Drainage Behind Retaining Walls

  • Water pressure is the #1 cause of retaining wall failure — drainage is critical
  • 12" minimum width of clean ¾" crushed stone behind the wall, full height
  • 4" perforated drain pipe at the base, sloped to daylight at ends
  • Landscape fabric separates native soil from drainage gravel to prevent clogging
  • Gravel volume: approximately 1 cu ft per linear foot per foot of wall height

Permit Requirements

  • Walls under 4 ft generally don't require a permit in most areas (check locally)
  • Walls 4 ft and taller typically require an engineered design and building permit
  • Walls near property lines may require setbacks — verify with your local zoning department
  • Walls supporting surcharge loads (driveways, structures) may require engineering regardless of height
  • Always call 811 before digging to locate underground utilities

Base Preparation

  • Excavate a level trench 6"–8" deep and 24" wide for the base course
  • Compact 4"–6" of crushed stone base material (not pea gravel)
  • First course should be partially buried for stability — at least half the block height
  • Each course should set back (batter) ¼" to ¾" for structural stability
