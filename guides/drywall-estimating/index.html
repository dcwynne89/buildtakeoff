Drywall Estimating: A Complete Guide for Contractors

June 2026 11 min read Drywall

Drywall is one of the highest-volume materials in residential construction, and estimating it accurately means the difference between a smooth job and scrambling for emergency deliveries. This guide covers everything from sheet selection to calculating mud, tape, and screws.

Drywall Sheet Sizes

Drywall sheets come in three standard widths (48 inches) and multiple lengths:

Estimating Sheet Count

Sheets Needed = Total Wall Area ÷ Sheet Size + Waste Factor

Step-by-Step Method

  1. Calculate total wall area: Measure the perimeter of each room and multiply by ceiling height. For a 12×14 room with 8' ceilings: (12+14+12+14) × 8 = 416 sq ft of wall.
  2. Add ceiling area if applicable: 12 × 14 = 168 sq ft. Total = 584 sq ft.
  3. Subtract openings (optional): Standard door = 21 sq ft. Standard window = 15 sq ft. Subtracting gives you a tighter quantity, but many estimators leave openings in to account for waste.
  4. Divide by sheet size: 584 ÷ 32 = 18.25 sheets (for 4×8). Round up to 19.
  5. Add waste: 19 × 1.10 = 20.9 → Order 21 sheets.

Choose sheet length to match ceiling height. For 8' ceilings, use 4×8 sheets hung horizontally — two sheets cover floor to ceiling with one horizontal joint. For 9' ceilings, use 4×10 sheets to eliminate a horizontal butt joint. Fewer joints = less taping and a smoother finish.

Joint Compound (Mud) Quantities

Joint compound is needed for taping joints, covering screws, and finishing corners. The amount depends on joint length and finish level:

Method Quantity Notes
Per 1,000 sq ft of drywall~3.5 gallons (one 4.5-gal bucket)Standard Level 4 finish
Per 4×8 sheet~0.07 gallonsIncluding 3 coats on joints and screws
Per linear foot of joint~0.04 gallonsAll three coats combined

Rule of thumb: One 4.5-gallon bucket of pre-mixed all-purpose joint compound covers approximately 1,000–1,200 sq ft of drywall for a standard Level 4 finish (tape, block coat, skim coat). For Level 5 finish (full skim coat), plan on 50% more.

Tape Quantities

  • Paper tape: Standard for most joints. A 500-foot roll costs $3–$6 and covers approximately 12–15 sheets of 4×8 drywall.
  • Mesh tape: Self-adhesive fiberglass mesh. Easier to apply but requires setting-type compound (not pre-mixed) for proper strength. Used primarily on butt joints and patches.
  • Estimation rule: You need approximately 40 linear feet of tape per 4×8 sheet (covering two long edges and any butt joints). For a room with 21 sheets, that's about 840 LF of tape — roughly 2 rolls of 500-foot paper tape.

Screw Calculations

Drywall screws should be placed every 12 inches along the edges of the sheet and every 16 inches in the field (center studs/joists):

Sheet Size Screws per Sheet Screw Type
4×8 on walls (16" OC studs)28–321-1/4" coarse thread (for wood framing)
4×8 on ceilings (16" OC joists)32–401-1/4" coarse thread (closer spacing on ceilings)
4×12 on walls40–481-1/4" coarse thread
5/8" Type X on walls28–321-5/8" coarse thread (longer for thicker board)
Double-layer (2nd layer)28–321-5/8" or 2" (must reach framing)

Quick math: Plan on 32 screws per 4×8 sheet as your default. A 1-lb box contains approximately 200 screws. So 21 sheets × 32 screws = 672 screws ≈ 4 lbs (1-lb boxes) or 1 five-pound box.

Corner Bead

Every outside corner needs corner bead. Measure the total linear feet of outside corners:

  • Paper-faced corner bead: $2–$4 per 8' or 10' stick. Embedded with joint compound. Less prone to denting than metal.
  • Metal corner bead: $1–$3 per 8' or 10' stick. Nailed or crimped in place. Classic and inexpensive.
  • Vinyl corner bead: $3–$5 per stick. Adhesive or stapled. Resistant to cracking.
  • Bull-nose bead: Rounded profile. $4–$8 per stick. Popular in modern/contemporary finishes.

Don't forget J-bead (for exposed drywall edges at windows, beams, etc.) and L-bead (for drywall meeting dissimilar materials).

Fire-Rated Assemblies

Building code requires fire-rated drywall assemblies in specific locations:

  • Attached garage walls/ceiling adjacent to living space: Minimum 1/2" Type X on garage side (many jurisdictions require 5/8" Type X)
  • Between dwelling units (duplexes, townhomes): 1-hour assembly minimum — typically 5/8" Type X each side of common wall
  • Furnace/boiler room enclosures: 5/8" Type X minimum
  • Stairwell enclosures in multi-story: 1-hour rated assembly
  • Commercial corridor walls: 1-hour to 2-hour ratings depending on occupancy

Fire-rated assemblies are system-rated, not just drywall-rated. The assembly includes the drywall type, stud type (wood vs steel), stud spacing, insulation, and fastener pattern. Using 5/8" Type X on non-rated framing spacing doesn't create a compliant fire-rated assembly. Always reference the specific UL assembly number specified in the plans.

Moisture-Resistant Drywall

Use moisture-resistant drywall in areas exposed to humidity (but NOT direct water contact):

  • Green board (moisture-resistant drywall): For bathroom walls NOT in the shower/tub surround area, laundry rooms, and kitchens. $14–$20 per 4×8 sheet.
  • Purple board (mold-resistant): Superior mold and moisture resistance. $16–$24 per sheet. Good choice for all bathrooms.
  • Cement board (Durock, Hardiebacker): Required behind tile in shower and tub surrounds. Not drywall, but often included in drywall scope. $10–$15 per 3×5 sheet.
  • DensShield/DensArmor: Fiberglass-faced tile backer. Combines moisture resistance with tile substrate. $14–$20 per sheet.

Standard drywall should never be used behind tile in wet areas. Even "moisture-resistant" green board is not approved for direct tile application in showers per most codes. Use cement board or a fiberglass-faced tile backer behind any tiled shower or tub surround.
