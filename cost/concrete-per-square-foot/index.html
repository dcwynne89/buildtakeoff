What Goes Into Concrete Cost Per Square Foot?

Concrete pricing isn't just "material + labor." Understanding each component helps you bid accurately and spot when a quote is too high — or suspiciously low.

Materials (~$5 – $7 per sq ft)

Ready-mix concrete typically runs $140 – $165 per cubic yard delivered in 2026. For a standard 4-inch slab, that translates to roughly $5 – $7 per square foot in material alone. Costs vary by mix design — a 4,000 PSI mix costs more than a standard 3,000 PSI residential mix.

Labor (~$3 – $6 per sq ft)

Labor covers forming, pouring, screeding, floating, and finishing. Simple broom-finish work runs $3 – $4 per square foot. Stamped or exposed-aggregate finishes push labor to $5 – $6+ due to additional skill and timing requirements.

Extras That Add Up

  • Formwork: $1 – $2/LF for standard 2×4 or 2×6 forms
  • Rebar / Wire Mesh: $0.50 – $1.50/sq ft depending on spacing and bar size
  • Vapor Barrier: $0.15 – $0.25/sq ft (10-mil poly)
  • Sub-base Gravel: $1 – $2/sq ft for a 4" compacted gravel base
  • Finishing: Broom finish is included; trowel/stamp/stain adds $2 – $8/sq ft
  • Pump Truck: $150 – $300 flat fee if the truck can't reach the pour site

Factors That Affect Concrete Pricing

Slab Thickness

Going from 4" to 6" adds 50% more material. A 20×20 ft slab at 4" requires ~4.9 cubic yards; at 6" it needs ~7.4 yards. Thickness is often the single biggest cost driver for slabs.

Site Conditions

Grade work, tree root removal, poor soil requiring over-excavation, and limited access for the concrete truck all add cost. Expect $1 – $3/sq ft for significant site prep on challenging lots.

Reinforcement

Wire mesh is cheapest ($0.50/sq ft) but least strong. #4 rebar at 12" on-center is standard for driveways ($1 – $1.50/sq ft). Post-tension slabs used in expansive soil areas cost $1.50 – $2.50/sq ft more.

Finish Type

  • Broom Finish: Standard, no additional cost
  • Trowel Finish: Adds $0.50 – $1/sq ft (smooth, indoor use)
  • Stamped: Adds $4 – $8/sq ft (decorative patterns)
  • Exposed Aggregate: Adds $3 – $6/sq ft
  • Acid Stain: Adds $2 – $5/sq ft

Time of Year

Concrete work in cold weather requires hot water, blankets, or accelerators — adding $1 – $2/sq ft. Summer is peak season with higher labor rates but ideal curing conditions.
